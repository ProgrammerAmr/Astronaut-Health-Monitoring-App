Study Identifier	OSD-431
Study Title	Microgravity and space radiation exert either opposite or synergic effects on single molecular pathways
Study Description	Microgravity and space radiation (SR) are two highly influential factors affecting humans in space flight. Many health problems reported by astronauts derive from endothelial dysfunction and impaired homeostasis. Here we describe the adaptive response of human, capillary endothelial cells to space. Reference samples on ground and at 1g onboard allowed discrimination between the contribution of microgravity and SR within the combined response to space. Cell softening and reduced motility occurred in space, with loss of actin stress fibers and a greater distribution of microtubules and intermediate filaments in compensation. The frequency of primary cilia also increased. DNA repair mechanisms were indeed activated. Transcriptomics highlighted the opposing effect of microgravity from SR on specific molecular pathways: radiation, unlike microgravity, stimulated pathways for endothelial activation (hypoxia, inflammation), DNA repair and apoptosis, promoting an ageing-like phenotype; microgravity, unlike SR, activated pathways for metabolism and pro-proliferation phenotype. Thus, microgravity and SR should be considered separately to tailor effective countermeasures to protect astronauts’ health. To identify transcriptional changes induced by microgravity, space radiation, and their combination together with other stimuli in space flight, we used a model of human endothelial cells of microvascular origin (HMEC-1). Space samples were exposed to environmental microgravity (g) or set inside the centrifuge reproducing 1g; on ground samples were used as ground control.

Study Protocol Description	HMEC-1 cells were seeded on Cyclic Olefin Copolymer coverslips, with 2% gelatin coating to improve cell adhesion, in MCDB131 medium supplemented with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum, hydrocortisone (1 ug/ml), EGF (10 ng/ml), 1 mM sodium bicarbonate, Penicillin (1 U/ml) Streptomycin (1 ug/ml), HEPES (20 mM). Cell cultures were incubated at 37degreeC in 5% CO2, 80% humidity, before insertion into the Experimental Units (EUs). The EUs are able to fulfill under electronic control and without human intervention, a scientific protocol for cell culture and fixing. 20 samples prepared for nucleic acid extraction were fixed with RNA Protect Cell Reagent.	Samples were brought to the Columbus module of the ISS with the Soyuz TMA-18M 44S manned mission on September 2nd, 2015, within the IRISS mission of ESA. At rendezvous, samples were immediately transferred into the KUBIC facility, an incubator with internal centrifuge for artificial gravity. Space microg samples were exposed to environmental microgravity (ug) and named Space_microg samples; Space_1g samples were set inside the centrifuge reproducing 1g. Other samples, concomitantly prepared for ground control, flew back to Pisa with civilian airlines just when the others were flying into space, and were integrated within the KISS facility in the home laboratory. Experiment activation and media exchanges occurred contemporarily in space and on ground.	At the end of the space mission, the samples were fixed in space, sent back to Earth with manned Soyuz TMA-16M 42S on September 12, 2015, retrieved by ESA personnel, brought to the PI laboratory in Pisa and stored at 6C until processed.	Total RNA was extracted with ReliaPrep RNA Cell Miniprep System (Promega, Madison WI, USA). RNA integrity was assessed with agarose gel electrophoresis and ethidium bromide staining/UV light (GenoPlex, VWR, USA), concentration and purity with NanoView Spectrophotometer (GE Healthcare, Pittsburgh, PA, USA).	The RNA-seq libraries were prepared using TruSeq Stranded Total RNA with Ribo-Zero Library Prep Kit.	The libraries were sequenced with Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform and CASAVA version 1.8.2 was used to perform base calling. The quality of the 2 × 125 bp and 2 × 130 bp paired-end reads was assessed with FastQC.	Raw reads were aligned using STAR version 2.5.3 to build version hg38 of the human genome defined by GENCODE release 25. Counts for GENCODE basic annotated genes were calculated from the aligned reads using featureCounts function of the Rsubread R package. Normalization was carried out using edgeR R package. After removal of genes encoded on mitochondrial chromosome, raw counts were normalized according to library size to obtain counts per millions (CPM). Only genes with a CPM greater than 1 in at least 2 sample were retained for subsequent analysis. Genome_build: hg38 (GRCh38)	Raw fastq files from each sample were assessed for % rRNA using HTStream SeqScreener (version 1.3.2) screened against a fasta file of Homo sapiens rRNA sequences retrieved from NCBI on 01-Sep-2021. Raw fastq file quality was evaluated with FastQC (version 0.12.1) and MultiQC (version 1.26) was used to generate MultiQC reports.	Data were processed as described in GL-DPPD-7101-G (https://github.com/nasa/GeneLab_Data_Processing/blob/master/RNAseq/Pipeline_GL-DPPD-7101_Versions/GL-DPPD-7101-G.md), using NF_RCP version 2.1.0 (https://github.com/nasa/GeneLab_RNASeq_Workflow/tree/NF_RCP_2.1.0). In short, raw fastq files were filtered using Trim Galore! (version 0.6.10) powered by Cutadapt (version 4.2). Trimmed fastq file quality was evaluated with FastQC (version 0.12.1), and MultiQC (version 1.26) was used to generate MultiQC reports. Homo Sapiens STAR and RSEM references were built using STAR (version 2.7.11b) and RSEM (version 1.3.3), respectively, Ensembl release 112, genome assembly GRCh38 (Homo_sapiens.GRCh38.dna.primary_assembly.fa) and the following gtf annotation file: Homo_sapiens.GRCh38.112.gtf. Trimmed reads were aligned to the Homo Sapiens STAR reference with STAR (version 2.7.11b) and alignment log files were compiled with MultiQC (version 1.26). Aligned reads were assessed for strandedness using RSeQC infer_experiment.py (version 5.0.4), gene body coverage using RSeQC geneBody_coverage.py (version 5.0.4), inner distance was assessed using RSeQC inner_distance.py (version 5.0.4), and distribution of RNA features using RSeQC read_distribution.py (version 5.0.4). Aligned reads from all samples were quantified using RSEM (version 1.3.3), with strandedness set to reverse and RSEM count log files were compiled with MultiQC (version 1.26). To create a set of rRNA-removed quantification data, Ensembl IDs mapping to ribosomal RNA (rRNA) were removed from the RSEM gene count data. Both the original set of RSEM quantification data and the rRNA-removed data were subject to differential gene expression analysis as follows. A runsheet was generated with dp_tools (version 1.3.8) and the runsheet and quantification data were imported to R (version 4.4.2) with tximport (version 1.34.0) and normalized with DESeq2 (version 1.46.0) median of ratios method. Normalized gene counts were subject to differential expression analysis. Differential expression analysis was performed in R (version 4.4.2) using DESeq2 (version 1.46.0); all groups were compared pairwise using the Wald test and the likelihood ratio test was used to generate the F statistic p-value. Gene annotations were assigned using the custom annotation tables generated in-house as detailed in GL-DPPD-7110-A (https://github.com/nasa/GeneLab_Data_Processing/blob/GL_RefAnnotTable-A_1.1.0/GeneLab_Reference_Annotations/Pipeline_GL-DPPD-7110_Versions/GL-DPPD-7110-A/GL-DPPD-7110-A.md), with STRINGdb (version 2.16.4), PANTHER.db (version 1.0.12), and org.Hs.eg.db (version 3.19.1).
